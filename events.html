sf ai event radar

The rooms worth being in, scored for a writer covering AI, China, and Silicon Valley. Last updated .

You keep missing the rooms that matter because the good ones never reach a public calendar: they travel through Luma approval lists, a host's WhatsApp group, a fellowship alumni email, or a friend's plus-one. This page is the fix. It is a curated, scored radar of the Bay Area's highest-signal AI events, salons, parties, and networks, each evaluated for one question: what does a writer covering AI, China, and Silicon Valley actually get from being there?

It is deliberately opinionated. Mega-conferences that generate press releases and little else are scored low or left out. Small rooms where the agenda is set are scored high even when they are hard to get into, because the path in is described for each one.

    how events are scored

    Signal density (35%)How many people who actually set the agenda are in the room, per square foot. A 40-person dinner with three lab founders beats a 10,000-person expo.
    Writer ROI (35%)Story material, quotable sources, editors and commissioning opportunities, distribution for Concurrent. Would a day here produce a piece, a source, or a relationship?
    China × SV relevance (15%)How directly the room feeds your beat: US–China AI, open models, chips, the diaspora founder scene, the "Industrial Party" mind.
    Exclusivity (15%)How hard it is to get in. Scarcity is a proxy for candor: people speak differently when the room is curated and off the record.

    Composite score is out of 5. Red bar = tier 1, plan the season around it. Blue bar = tier 2, go if the timing works. Grey = worth knowing about. Dates marked unverified follow the event's usual pattern and need confirming on the official page.

    how a writer gets into rooms

    Nearly every event above has a side door that is not the ticket page. In rough order of how well they work:

    1. Cover it. Press credentials are the cheapest ticket in the city. TechCrunch Disrupt, Cerebral Valley (media passes are free), OpenAI DevDay, NVIDIA GTC, AI Engineer, and most conferences of any size credential freelancers with two recent bylines. Lead with WIRED every time. Keep a current Muck Rack profile with the beat set to "AI, US–China, San Francisco": publicists build their invite lists from it, and that is how invite-only dinners find you. A National Writers Union press pass is a cheap fallback credential.
    2. Be on stage. You have already moderated at Asia Society, DevCon, and Stanford. A speaker or moderator slot converts to invitations everywhere else. Standing pitches to keep live: "Chinese open-weight models and what the Valley gets wrong" (Cerebral Valley, AI Engineer, Semafor, GPTDAO), "China's Industrial Party and American techno-optimism" (Progress Conference, LessOnline, Interintellect), "the diaspora AI founder wave" (HYSTA, Asia Society).
    3. Use the fellowships. GovAI and Roots of Progress are the two credentials that open Lighthaven, Constellation, EA Global, and Progress Conference. Email the alumni channels rather than the public application form; ask for the alumni invitation by name.
    4. Convene. The fastest way to get invited is to invite. A recurring eight-person Concurrent dinner (one lab person, one China-tech founder, one policy person, one editor, one skeptic) costs an evening a month and makes you a node rather than a guest. Interintellect and The Commons will host and promote a salon if you would rather not run logistics.
    5. Ask for the list. Every organizer (Cerebral Valley, Founders Inc, Frontier Tower, the Lighthaven crew, Tech Week hosts) keeps a WhatsApp or Slack group that never hits Luma. When you meet one, say you are a journalist who covers the scene and ask to be added. They hold press and plus-one slots for sold-out events.
    6. Anchor the year on Lighthaven. The Berkeley campus hosts the season's spine: The Curve and Progress Conference in October, EA Global in spring, LessOnline and Manifest in June, EAGx in August. Being a regular there is worth more than any single conference badge.

    signal sources

    Where events surface before they fill. Most Luma calendars have a Subscribe → Add iCal button; pipe those into a grey "Radar" calendar so the machine layer runs itself, and keep the human layer (the X thread, the newsletters, Partiful) for a Sunday skim. Partiful has no feed at all, so the parties stay manual.

    the weekly routine

    1. Once (30 minutes): add the iCal feeds above to a separate "Radar" calendar in Google Calendar. Subscribe to Cerebral Valley, Stochastic Sandbox, ChinaTalk, Tech Buzz China, DigiChina, and Asia Society emails. Set up a Muck Rack profile.
    2. Sunday (20 minutes): read Michelle Fang's thread, the Cerebral Valley issue, and Partiful Explore. RSVP the same day to anything with a cap or approval gate; those fill in 24 to 72 hours.
    3. Daily (5 minutes): skim the Latent Space and Frontier Tower Discord event channels and an X list of organizers. Invite-only dinners are announced there first, usually as "DM for invite."
    4. Monthly: check the Lighthaven schedule and this page's "act now" list. Send press or speaker requests six to eight weeks before anything tier 1 or tier 2.
    5. Quarterly: host one dinner. Update this page: rescore what disappointed, add what you heard about too late, and note who got you in.
